NASA (National Aeronautics and Space Administration) is the United States government agency responsible for civilian space exploration, aeronautics research, and space science. It was established in 1958 in response to the early space race and has since led major missions in human spaceflight, planetary exploration, Earth observation, and astrophysics.

Key roles of NASA include:

Designing and launching spacecraft and satellites

Conducting scientific research about Earth, the solar system, and the universe

Operating human spaceflight programs (e.g., Apollo Moon missions, Space Shuttle, International Space Station)

Developing advanced aerospace technologies

NASA’s work has contributed to technologies such as satellite communications, GPS, weather forecasting, and scientific understanding of space and Earth.
